The WordPress problem

WordPress is flexible. That is exactly why it needs discipline.

Most WordPress sites become hard to manage because every change adds another plugin, builder block, or exception. The work is making the site easier to edit without making it weaker to crawl, slower to load, or harder to trust.

What we clean up

  • Theme bloat and fragile page-builder patterns.
  • Plugin stacks that slow the first screen or create update risk.
  • Unclear service hierarchy, weak internal links, and thin pages.
  • Forms, tracking, and CTAs that do not support lead quality.
  • Schema and metadata that do not match visible content.

What we build in

  • Custom templates for service, blog, location, tool, and proof pages.
  • Editor-safe components your team can reuse without breaking layout.
  • SEO/AEO-ready headings, FAQs, schema, and internal-link paths.
  • Performance work around images, fonts, caching, scripts, and hosting.
  • Analytics events for forms, audit usage, downloads, and contact paths.

Process

A build process that protects search, speed, and editing.

We start with structure before visuals: what pages exist, what should rank, what should convert, what the team needs to edit, and what should never be editable by accident.

  1. Audit the current site. Templates, plugins, crawlability, Core Web Vitals, analytics, forms, content depth, schema, and lead paths.
  2. Map the content system. Service pages, blog structure, location paths, case studies, FAQs, tools, and editorial rules.
  3. Design reusable patterns. Blocks and templates that look polished but stay hard to misuse.
  4. Build for performance. Lean theme code, restrained plugins, optimized media, caching, and clean script loading.
  5. Handoff with guardrails. Editor roles, documentation, analytics checks, redirects, sitemap, and post-launch QA.
WordPress FAQ

Short answers before you rebuild.

Should I choose WordPress, Webflow, Shopify, or a custom site? +
Choose WordPress when publishing flexibility, content depth, WooCommerce, or mature integrations matter. Choose Webflow when brand control and editor simplicity matter most. Choose Shopify for commerce. Choose custom when the business logic is the product.
Can WordPress be SEO and AEO friendly? +
Yes. The platform can support strong SEO/AEO when templates have clear headings, answer blocks, schema, fast loading, internal links, and useful content. The problem is usually implementation discipline, not WordPress itself.
Do you work with existing WordPress teams? +
Yes. Lofts Studio can audit the site, clean high-risk templates, improve speed, strengthen SEO structure, or build specific sections while an internal or external team keeps daily ownership.
