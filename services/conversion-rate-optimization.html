The Pattern

Most CRO fails because it skips the boring part.

Teams jump to "let's A/B test the button color" before they've understood why people are bouncing. The real lifts come from rewriting the buying decision — not from optimizing the wrapper around a bad decision.

Common CRO mistakes

  • —A/B tests on under-trafficked pages — never reach significance
  • —"Best practice" PDPs that fight your actual buyer's objections
  • —Cart abandonment "fixes" that ignore why the cart got abandoned
  • —Optimization without analytics — no baseline, no measurement
  • —Testing tools (VWO, Convert, AB Tasty) running but no real tests live

What I deliver

  • +Audit grounded in your actual session recordings + funnel data
  • +PDP rewrite around objections specific to your buyer
  • +Checkout flow tuned for the 68% who quit between cart and pay
  • +A/B tests structured to reach significance (or honest "we can't")
  • +Quarterly ongoing support option for ongoing testing program
The CRO Stack

Three layers. Where lift actually comes from.

Diagnostic Audit

GA4 + Hotjar/Microsoft Clarity + heatmap analysis. Where do visitors drop off? What sections kill scroll? What objection makes them leave? Output: a prioritized fix list with expected impact estimates.

GA4 · Clarity · Hotjar

PDP & Checkout Rewrite

The two pages that earn or lose conversion. PDP rebuilt around objection-handling, not feature listing. Checkout shortened, distractions removed, trust signals placed where buyers actually look.

PDP · Cart · Checkout

A/B Test Program

Real tests on pages that actually have traffic to reach significance. Bayesian or frequentist analysis depending on context. Winners shipped, losers learned from.

VWO · Convert · GrowthBook

FAQs

CRO questions, answered.

What conversion rate lift can I expect? +
Depends on baseline. Stores below 1.5% CR usually see 30–60% relative lift from the first round of fixes. Stores above 3% CR see 10–25%. Below 0.5% means there's a deeper product or scoping problem CRO can't fix — I'll tell you that on the audit call.
How much traffic do I need? +
For audit + fix work, anything above 5K monthly visitors. For A/B testing to reach statistical significance in reasonable time, you need ~25K+ visitors/month on the page you're testing. Below that, qualitative session-recording work beats split testing.
What does CRO effort? +
Scoped for the audit + first implementation sprint, with an optional ongoing testing ongoing support. You get the exact plan after the audit call — no open-ended billing.
Do you handle the design + dev or just strategy? +
Both. I personally design and code every change I recommend. No hand-off losses, no "your dev team will need to implement this" gaps. The same person doing strategy ships the fix.
How is this different from your Speed Optimization service? +
Speed is the foundation — slow stores can't convert. CRO is what comes after the floor is solid. If you're at <90 Lighthouse, fix speed first. If you're already fast but stuck on conversion, CRO is the right next step.
