Lead-path checklist

Service website audit checklist: can a visitor become a lead?

Start here: on a phone, can a new visitor understand the service, audience, proof, service area, and next action within the first screen? Then test the full path from search result to phone call, form, WhatsApp, booking, confirmation, and follow-up.

1. First-screen decision

CheckPass conditionIf it fails
OfferThe visitor can name what you do and who it is for.Rewrite the H1 and support copy around one buyer decision.
ProofA relevant project, credential, process, or verifiable signal is visible.Bring the strongest truthful proof closer to the claim.
ActionOne primary CTA is visible and specific.Choose the best next step: call, audit, booking, quote, or enquiry.
Mobile fitNo clipped text, overlap, horizontal scroll, or hidden action.Reduce density and preserve a stable, tappable first screen.

2. Phone, form, WhatsApp, and booking paths

  • Phone links use a valid tel: destination and show the correct business number.
  • WhatsApp opens the intended account with useful prefilled context, without covering another floating control.
  • The form accepts realistic names, emails, phone formats, and domains without requiring visitors to type a protocol.
  • Required fields, errors, loading, success, duplicate-submit prevention, and keyboard focus are clear.
  • The success state explains what happens next and triggers the correct analytics event only after a confirmed submission.
  • Bookings preserve timezone, availability, confirmation, and rescheduling information.

3. Search result and landing-page match

Compare the search phrase, title, description, H1, first answer, and CTA. A visitor searching for a report, template, checker, or audit should receive that useful thing immediately, not a generic agency introduction. Each important page should have one canonical URL and a distinct job.

4. Trust and proof

SignalWhat to verify
IdentityBusiness name, people, contact route, and public profiles are consistent.
WorkCase studies distinguish shipped work from concepts and do not overstate outcomes.
ProcessScope, responsibilities, feedback, launch, and post-launch expectations are understandable.
PoliciesPrivacy, cookies, terms, accessibility, and consent language match actual behavior.
ClaimsReviews, ratings, locations, experience, and performance statements are supportable.

5. Answer-engine and content clarity

  • Define the service, audience, constraints, and next action in a concise answer block.
  • Use descriptive headings and FAQs that mirror visible buyer questions.
  • Link the page to the most relevant service, tool, proof, and contact path.
  • Use schema only when it accurately describes the visible organization, service, article, breadcrumb, or FAQ content.

6. Follow-up and measurement

Confirm that qualified enquiries reach the right inbox or CRM, contain enough context, and have an owner. Measure form starts, confirmed submissions, phone or WhatsApp clicks, bookings, source, landing page, and lead quality. Traffic alone cannot tell you whether the website is doing its job.

Priority order

  1. Blockers: broken pages, blocked indexing, failed forms, wrong contact destinations, or unsafe behavior.
  2. Lead-path friction: unclear offer, invisible CTA, weak mobile flow, or missing confirmation.
  3. Trust and intent: proof gaps, search mismatch, vague answers, or unsupported claims.
  4. Optimization: performance, internal links, structured data, and lower-impact polish.

FAQ

What should a service website audit check first?

Check whether the first screen explains the service, audience, proof, service area, and next action on mobile. Then test the complete phone, form, WhatsApp, booking, and follow-up path.

How do you prioritize service website audit findings?

Fix broken access and lead paths first, then unclear offers, missing proof, search and answer clarity, performance, and lower-impact polish. Assign an owner and validation step to every finding.
