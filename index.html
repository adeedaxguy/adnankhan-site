01 On craft
“Average launches fast and dies quiet.”
Written by Adnan Khan
Filed June 2026
Reading 3 min
Provendelivery history
15 yrshands-on experience

Most websites are theater. The tickets close. The numbers don’t move.

I have audited a lot of sites — stores, platforms, and custom apps. The pattern is almost always the same — a thirty-page Notion of “requirements,” a six-week build that drifted into ten, a launch announcement, and then a quiet, slow slide back to whatever the template gives you out of the box. The buttons are different. The fundamentals aren’t.

What founders bought was activity. What they wanted was a site that compounds — that loads in under two seconds on the cheapest Android phone in your customer’s pocket, that survives the day a homepage hero video gets indexed by Google, that holds its conversion rate when Meta’s CPMs double.

The senior version has stopped pretending the templates know what your business looks like. The junior version is still treating “launched” as the finish line, when in fact launched is the first day of the work.

I am betting almost 15 years of work on the difference. Everything on this page — the work, the reviews, the way I write — is what that bet looks like up close.

Questions every founder asks first

The honest answers, in order of how often I hear them.

Why work with one person instead of an agency? +
Because agencies bill you for the project manager, the account director, and the junior developer who actually writes the code — and then hand it to that junior. Working with me, you get the same person on every line of code, every commit, every Loom. No layers, no markup, no junior handoffs. The trade-off: I take fewer projects. Most of the year my calendar is one or two slots out.
Can you really launch in two weeks? +
For a build with a clear scope — a store, a marketing site, or a custom app — yes — and I’ve done it three hundred times. The trick isn’t speed. It’s a battle-tested process and the discipline to say no to things that belong in v2. We’ll define exactly what ships first, and everything else moves to a clear roadmap. No scope creep is the actual product.
What does a typical project effort? +
Every project is scope-led to exactly what you need — no open-ended billing, no surprises. You get an exact plan after a short strategy call, with milestones written into project workspace before any work starts. Most clients find it comes in well under an agency proposal for the same scope.
Do you work with US and UK clients remotely? +
Exclusively. About ninety percent of my work is US or UK founders. Async-first with daily Loom updates, every milestone documented and milestone-led via Upwork. There is no contract risk on your end — that’s how the hundred-percent JSS got there.
What happens after the site goes live? +
Every project includes thirty days of post-launch support — bug fixes, real-traffic tuning, performance maintenance. After that most clients move to a lightweight ongoing support plan so I’m on call for new sections, A/B tests, and seasonal launches. About a third of my work in any given month is for a client I built for last year, or the year before.
Why is your site so quiet about “hire me”? +
Because I’d rather be the person you remembered when you needed work that lasts, than the person you bought from because the CTA was loud. If the writing here resonates, you already know where the form is. If it doesn’t, you saved both of us a call.
