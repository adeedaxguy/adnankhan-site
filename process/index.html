Direct delivery · visible decisions

A process that keeps the work and the reasoning together

I stay responsible from the first scope through the final release. The process is designed to expose decisions early, keep feedback specific, and prevent launch-week surprises.

In brief

Every engagement moves through six visible phases: frame, map, design, implement, verify, and hand off. The depth changes with the project; the responsibility for clarity and QA does not.

Six phases

From unclear brief to a controlled release

Each phase produces something reviewable before the next one takes on more cost or technical risk.

  1. 01Frame the decision

    Clarify the audience, business action, evidence, constraints, and launch condition before choosing screens or technology.

  2. 02Map the experience

    Turn the brief into page hierarchy, content requirements, user states, integrations, and a reviewable first scope.

  3. 03Design with real content

    Build the responsive interface around representative copy, media, products, forms, and edge states rather than empty boxes.

  4. 04Implement in small releases

    Keep working software visible on staging, document tradeoffs, and resolve platform risks before they reach launch week.

  5. 05QA the complete path

    Test navigation, content, forms or commerce, responsive behavior, accessibility basics, metadata, and error states.

  6. 06Launch and hand off

    Release with a clear checklist, ownership notes, measurement requirements, and a record of anything intentionally deferred.

What keeps it moving

Small feedback loops, one approval owner.

A project moves faster when the source material, technical access, and decision owner are known. I document open questions and tradeoffs so feedback can resolve a decision instead of restarting the design.

When a specialist or client-side dependency is required, it is made visible in the scope rather than hidden behind an agency promise.

Useful inputs

  • Current site, product, or working brief
  • Audience and intended customer action
  • Content, brand, and proof assets
  • Platform, integration, and access constraints
  • One owner for approvals and consolidated feedback
